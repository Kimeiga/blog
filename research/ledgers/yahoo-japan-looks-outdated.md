# Source ledger: Yahoo Japan looks outdated. It may be the better homepage.

Accessed 2026-09-04.

## Reader promise

Separate three things that are often collapsed into one argument about Japanese web design: visual complexity, information density, and navigation breadth. Use Yahoo Japan and Yahoo US as the concrete comparison, then ask what the experimental evidence actually supports.

## Hakan's position and trigger

- Trigger: https://youtube.com/shorts/GObkBWa9RYI
- Hakan opened Yahoo Japan and Yahoo US on mobile and supplied screenshots.
- Initial judgment: Yahoo Japan looks older but appears more useful because more services and status information are visible upfront.
- Uncertainty to preserve: whether this makes Japanese web design generally better, and whether research supports the difference.

## Sources and claims

| Source | Role / supported claim |
| --- | --- |
| https://www.lycorp.co.jp/ja/service/ | Primary source for the breadth of current Yahoo Japan / LY services, including News, Finance, real estate, Shopping, Auctions, Travel, transit, Maps, disaster alerts, Weather, Mail and Q&A. |
| https://services.yahoo.co.jp/ | Primary Yahoo Japan service directory, grouped into entertainment, news, commerce, maps/transport, payments/finance, travel and related categories. |
| https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN36655.html | Primary Yahoo US help page describing homepage features such as leading news, For You recommendations, trending searches, weather, horoscopes, stocks and sports scores. Used to avoid claiming those services do not exist just because they are absent from Hakan's first viewport. |
| https://www.yahooinc.com/press/yahoo-introduces-myscout-the-first-personalized-homepage-for-ai-answers | Primary source for March 2026 MyScout product direction: customizable homepage tiles spanning inbox, stocks, weather, sports, shopping and other Yahoo products. |
| https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376849 | Baughan et al., CHI 2020, N=165. Higher website visual complexity reduced search efficiency and information recall; effect varied with users' complexity preferences. |
| https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2009.04.002 | Tuch et al., 2009, N=48. Greater visual complexity was associated with slower visual search, worse recognition and more negative affective responses. |
| https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003 | Tuch et al., 2012. Low visual complexity and high prototypicality produced better aesthetic judgments; effects appeared at very short exposures including 17 ms. |
| https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2003.10.010 | Parush & Yuviler-Gavish, 2004, N=96. Broad, shallow navigation outperformed deeper navigation on both cellular-phone and desktop interfaces. |
| https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20382372/ | Ziefle, 2010, N=40 older participants. Navigation was best with large fonts plus a large preview; the worst condition combined a large font with only one visible function. Supports a tradeoff between readability and orientation rather than a simple less-is-better rule. |
| https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445519 | Baughan et al., CHI 2021, Japanese N=65, US N=84. Did not support the hypothesis that Japanese participants find/remember contextual information faster; Japanese participants were slower overall and the gap increased with complexity. |
| https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-51549-2_66 | Doi & Murata, 2020. Heuristic evaluation of nine matched company websites found more usability drawbacks on Japanese versions, especially the aesthetic/minimalist-design heuristic. Expert heuristic evidence, not a user-performance test. |
| https://www.japantimes.co.jp/life/2025/12/15/style-design/japan-internet-web-design/ | Reported interviews on Japanese web-design norms, risk aversion and legacy-feature accumulation. Reports Shoin Wolfe's LIFULL HOME'S test in which pared-down versions saw lower engagement and conversions and were reverted. This is reported production evidence, not a published controlled experiment. Also documents accessibility problems from text baked into images. |
| https://www.rieti.go.jp/en/publications/summary/09010001.html | 2009 RIETI study of Japan's multilayered software subcontracting industry. Independent software firms had higher TFP than prime/intermediate/final contractor groups. Supports the existence and productivity relevance of the structure, not a causal claim about Yahoo Japan's visual design. |

## Claims explicitly avoided

- No claim that Japanese web design is generally or scientifically proven better.
- No claim that Japanese users process dense pages faster because of kanji or culture.
- No claim that subcontracting caused Yahoo Japan's density.
- No claim that Yahoo US lacks weather, sports or finance, only that those were not exposed in Hakan's captured first viewport.
- No claim that the reported LIFULL HOME'S product test was a randomized A/B test because the public reporting does not establish the exact experimental design.
