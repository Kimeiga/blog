# Editorial style: useful enough to deserve the reader’s time

The target is not “human-looking AI text.” The target is authored work: Hakan’s question, Hakan’s judgment, verifiable evidence, and an edit that respects the reader.

## The six tests

Every article should pass these before publication:

1. **Specificity:** Could this opening belong only to this article and this author?
2. **Tension:** What remains genuinely uncertain, disputed, costly, or surprising?
3. **Evidence:** Which sentences are observations, which are sources, and which are inferences?
4. **Synthesis:** What does the article connect that the source list alone does not?
5. **Usefulness:** What can a reader decide, build, notice, or question afterward?
6. **Compression:** Which paragraph merely repeats the shape of the previous paragraph?

## Hakan’s useful native tendencies

The older archive is direct and question-led. It names projects, people, places, products, and embarrassing or unresolved thoughts. It often begins with a practical irritation instead of a grand thesis. Keep those properties. Do not sand them into generic authority.

## Patterns to cut

- ceremonial openings about a “rapidly evolving landscape”
- inflated historical importance unsupported by the evidence
- symmetrical three-part lists created only because three feels complete
- “not just X, but Y” as a reflex
- a recap paragraph after every section
- conclusion headings that merely restate the introduction
- generic abstractions where a named system, price, street, commit, conversation, or constraint exists
- em-dash clusters, boldface confetti, and constant mini-headings
- an omniscient first person that implies Hakan personally ran research or witnessed events he did not

## Structure

Outline around questions and pressure points, not around a school-essay template. A strong technical or analytical article often moves like this:

- a concrete problem or observation
- the tempting simple answer
- the evidence that breaks it
- the model that explains more
- tradeoffs and failed alternatives
- what changes in practice

Do not force this shape when the material wants another one.

## Source discipline

Create a claim ledger while researching:

| Claim | Type | Source | Stability | Article wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What happened | reported fact | primary/authoritative | changing/stable | direct statement |
| Why it happened | interpretation | multiple sources | contested | attributed or qualified |
| What follows | Hakan’s inference | supporting evidence | uncertain | explicitly framed as inference |

Direct quotation is rare. Prefer accurate paraphrase and link to the source. Dates, prices, laws, releases, office-holders, and product behavior must be rechecked at publication time.

## Editing passes

1. **Argument pass:** remove contradictions, missing steps, and fake certainty.
2. **Fact pass:** verify every proper noun, number, date, quotation, and current product fact.
3. **Voice pass:** restore concrete nouns, reactions, humor, mixed feelings, and sentence rhythm.
4. **Slop pass:** remove generic transitions, over-signposting, repetition, and over-neat endings.
5. **Read-aloud pass:** break sentences that require a second breath; combine staccato anti-slop fragments.
6. **Title/dek pass:** promise exactly what the article delivers.

AI detectors are not an acceptance test. They can be bypassed and can falsely classify human writing, especially writing by non-native English speakers. Quality is judged against the article and its evidence.
